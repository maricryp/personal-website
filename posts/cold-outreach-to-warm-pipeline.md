Cold outreach gets a bad reputation because most of it is lazy — the same template, sent to a thousand people, with a name field swapped in. It doesn't have to work that way. Here's the playbook I actually run.

## 1. Narrow the list before you write a single message

I'd rather have 30 accounts I understand than 300 I don't. For each one, I want to know: what changed for them recently (funding, leadership, product launch), and why that change makes this the right moment to talk.

## 2. Lead with their problem, not my pitch

The first message never mentions what I sell. It names something specific about their situation and asks a real question. If I can't write that sentence, the account isn't ready yet — I wait, or I move on.

## 3. Multi-thread early

One contact who goes quiet kills the deal. I look for a second and third person in the account within the first two weeks, not after the first one stalls.

## 4. Follow up with new information, not just a nudge

"Just following up" adds nothing. A relevant article, a customer story from their industry, an answer to a question they didn't ask yet — that's a reason to reply.

## 5. Qualify out loud

Once there's a real conversation, I say plainly what would make this a good fit and what wouldn't. Prospects trust people who aren't just trying to close them.

This isn't a growth hack. It's slower than blasting a list, and it produces a pipeline I'm not embarrassed to forecast.
